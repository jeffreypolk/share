Chapter II

Know before you go

Practical notes · Weather · Getting around

Dublin is small, walkable, and forgiving. A few things worth sorting before you land.

Waterproof layers, real shoes

Fall = wind and drizzle, especially at Howth on the cliffs. A windbreaker won't cut it — bring something genuinely waterproof, plus shoes broken in for lots of walking.

Grab a Leap Card

Available at any DART/bus station or the airport, ~€5 refundable deposit. Makes buses, DART, and Luas one-tap. Essential for the Sunday Howth trip.

Cash is optional

Contactless (Apple/Google Pay, tap-to-pay) works everywhere — pubs, cabs, buses, corner shops. A little cash for tips is nice but you don't need to hit an ATM.

Tipping is casual

10% at sit-down restaurants if service was good. Rounding up in pubs and cabs is fine. Nobody expects US-style tipping.

Airport to Wynn's

Taxi is easiest with six people and luggage (~€25–30 total, 30 min). The Airlink 747 bus stops at Connolly Station right next to the hotel, €7 pp, 30 min, every 15 minutes.

Wynn's location is a superpower

Lower Abbey Street. 8-min walk to Trinity, 3-min walk to Connolly Station for the Sunday DART to Howth. Almost everything on this itinerary is walkable from the hotel.

Chapter III · Day One

Friday · arrival

Land 8:30 AM · Ease in · 1916 history in the afternoon

The trick with jet lag is daylight and motion. Don't sit down in a dark museum for too long — you'll fall asleep. Coffee, walk, one strict nap (30 min, two alarms), the GPO museum at 3, hearty dinner, one pub, bed by 10:30.

FriArrival

Foggy morning, 1916 stories

A low-pressure day. The GPO Witness History museum in the afternoon is a great jet-lag activity — self-paced, engaging without demanding sharp attention, and it puts the whole Easter Rising story in your head before we see Dublin proper on Saturday.

~10:00 AM Arrive at Wynn's · drop bags

Reception will hold your bags until check-in (standard everywhere). Don't try to check in until mid-afternoon — rooms rarely ready before then. Taxi from the airport is easiest for a group with luggage.

10:30 AM Real breakfast at a proper café

Not the airport. Options: Bewley's on Grafton Street (Dublin institution, atmospheric), Meet Me in the Morning on Pleasants Street (best coffee in the neighborhood), or Brother Hubbard on Capel Street (hearty, close to Wynn's). Sit for an hour.

Noon Easy walk · Grafton, Stephen's Green, Trinity grounds

Cross O'Connell Bridge, wander down Grafton Street, sit in St. Stephen's Green. You can walk Trinity's campus free without a ticket — save Book of Kells for Saturday. Duck into the Iveagh Gardens behind Stephen's Green — hidden and gorgeous.

1:00 PM Light lunch

Soup and bread at Fumbally, sandwiches at Meet Me in the Morning, or a bakery bite at Bread 41. Nothing heavy — you'll want to nap.

2:30 PM Check in · shower · strict 30-min nap (optional)

Set two alarms. Longer than 30 minutes and you're wrecked for the rest of the day.

3:00 PM · ~2 hrs GPO Witness History · O'Connell Street

5-min walk from Wynn's. Excellent museum inside the working General Post Office — the actual headquarters of the 1916 Easter Rising. Bullet holes still visible in the columns outside. Covers the Rising, the executions, the road to independence. Immersive, moving, and the perfect substitute for Kilmainham. Roughly €15, plan ~90 min to 2 hrs. Afterward, walk 10 minutes to the Garden of Remembrance for a quiet moment.

7:00 PM The Winding Stair · dinner

4-min walk across Ha'penny Bridge from Wynn's. Cozy Irish restaurant upstairs above a bookshop, Liffey views, hearty comfort food. Perfect Friday spot — atmospheric but not overwhelming.

9:00 PM O'Donoghues · trad music & one pint

15-min walk or 8-min taxi to Merrion Row. Save the Cobblestone for Saturday when you have energy. If you're truly wiped, The Palace Bar on Fleet Street (8 min away, Victorian atmosphere) or Mulligan's on Poolbeg Street (10 min, arguably the best-poured Guinness in the city) are excellent closer alternatives.

10:30 PM Back to Wynn's · sleep hard

Resist the urge to push later. Tomorrow is the heavy day.

Chapter IV · Day Two

Saturday · the big day

Trinity 9:30 AM · Guinness 2:30 PM · Vintage Kitchen 8:30 PM · Cobblestone late

The heavy hitter. Ancient books in the morning, a brewery pilgrimage in the afternoon, the best dinner and the best pub of the trip at night. Pace yourself — this is a full day.

SatCity Day

Book of Kells, a brewery, a feast

Trinity in the morning while everyone's fresh, an early lunch, Guinness in the afternoon (the tour is self-guided so no schedule pressure), a rest, then dinner and music. Wear good shoes.

9:30 AM · 90 min Trinity College & the Book of Kells

8-min walk from Wynn's down O'Connell Street and across the bridge. The Long Room library is the money shot — 200,000 old books on floor-to-ceiling shelves. Get there right at your slot; mid-morning gets crushed by tour groups.

11:30 AM Coffee & a wander

Coffee at Kaph or 3fe nearby. If the weather holds, walk to Merrion Square to see the Oscar Wilde statue and the famous Georgian doors.

12:30 PM Lunch · pick one

Bunsen (best burger in Dublin, tiny menu, fast in-and-out — good for keeping the group on schedule) · The Woollen Mills (traditional Irish, near Ha'penny Bridge, more seating for six) · Klaw (casual seafood, killer crab claws).

2:30 PM · 2.5 hrs Guinness Storehouse

10-min taxi (or two taxis for six) from lunch. Seven floors of brewing history, all self-guided so no pressure to keep pace with a tour. The tasting experience halfway up is fun, and it ends at the Gravity Bar on the top floor with 360° city views and your included pint. Book a specific entry slot online — mid-afternoon is the sweet spot after morning tour groups clear out.

5:30 PM Back to Wynn's · rest & freshen up

Long taxi ride back or hop the Luas red line from James's stop. Nap, shower, change. You've got time before dinner.

8:30 PM The Vintage Kitchen · dinner ✓

5-min walk from Wynn's across the river on Poolbeg Street. Small, intimate, modern Irish — mismatched vintage china, no website charm, exceptional value for the quality. The chef is often out saying hello. A special night out. Meet in the Wynn's lobby around 8:15 for the short walk over.

10:45 PM The Cobblestone · Smithfield

Post-dinner, taxi over (~8 min). The trad music pub locals swear by — musicians are friends jamming, not performers. Sessions run late; you'll walk in mid-flow. No food, just pints and music. If it's too packed, The Piper's Corner and Hughes' Bar nearby are excellent alternatives with the same energy.

Chapter V · Day Three

Sunday · Howth

DART to the coast · Castle tour · Cliff walk · Seafood

Wynn's superpower: 3-minute walk to Connolly Station for the DART to Howth. Leave the city behind, breathe sea air, tour a castle, walk cliffs, eat fish that was in the water this morning.

SunHowth

Coast day · cliffs & seafood

The tour is booked for 1 PM, so the day builds toward it. Morning: wander the village and harbour. Afternoon: castle, then the famous cliff walk. Evening: a proper seafood dinner at the pier.

8:30 AM Breakfast at Wynn's or nearby café

Fuel up. You'll be outside most of the day.

10:00 AM DART from Connolly Station

3-min walk from Wynn's. Tap on with your Leap Card, ~€3.50 each way. Trains every 15–20 min. Sit on the right side heading out — sea views for the last 10 minutes are worth it.

10:30 AM Arrive Howth · wander the harbour

Two hours before the castle tour. Walk the harbour, poke through the Howth Sunday Market (best day for it — food, crafts, local vendors). Grab coffee and a pastry at The Pier, Beshoff Bros, or Country Bake.

11:30 AM · 30 min Short warmup · East Pier walk (optional)

Easy 30-min out-and-back to the lighthouse. Great sea views, low effort. Just enough to get the legs moving before the castle tour.

12:30 PM Walk to Howth Castle

10-min walk from the village along the coast road.

1:00 PM · ~75 min Howth Castle · guided tour ✓

The booked tour. Should wrap around 2:15 PM.

2:30 PM · ~90 min Cliff Walk · Green Route

Trailhead is a short walk from the castle grounds. 6 km loop with the best sea views in the Dublin area — dramatic cliffs, wildflowers, the Baily Lighthouse in the distance. Pack layers, wear real shoes. The Green Route is the easy one; the Purple and Red are longer and steeper.

4:30 PM Seafood dinner at the harbour

Octopussy's Seafood Tapas (local favorite, freshest fish, group-friendly) · The Oar House (classic fish & chips, harbour views) · Aqua (splurge, best view, on the pier — call ahead for a party of six on a Sunday) · King Sitric (old-school Howth institution, more formal).

6:30 PM DART back to Connolly · 3 min home

Wynn's is right there.

8:00 PM One last pint

Mulligan's on Poolbeg Street (10 min from Wynn's) is a perfect final Dublin memory — arguably the best Guinness pour in the city, unchanged in a century. Or The Brazen Head (Ireland's oldest pub, since 1198) if you want the historical box ticked. Back by 11.

Chapter VII

Restaurants & pubs

Backups & alternatives · If plans shift

The itinerary above covers the essentials. Here's the wider list — extra dinner options if reservations fall through, more pubs than you can hit in one weekend, and a few skip-list warnings.

Restaurants worth knowing

If your first pick is full or you want to swap

Splurge · Michelin
Chapter One

12-min walk from Wynn's up Parnell Square. One-star Michelin, ambitious modern Irish, one of the best meals in Ireland. Book weeks ahead if you want it for Saturday.

Modern · Inventive
Mr Fox

Underground spot near Parnell Square. Playful, creative Irish cooking. Easier reservation than Chapter One, still special.

Casual · Near Wynn's
The Woollen Mills

3-min walk. All-day dining, hearty Irish comfort food, right at Ha'penny Bridge. Reliable backup for Friday or a lazy brunch.

Mediterranean · Near Wynn's
101 Talbot

4-min walk. Been around forever, Mediterranean-Irish menu, reliable and reasonably priced.

Small Plates · Wine
Etto

Merrion Row. Small plates and wine-bar vibe, Italian-leaning. Perfect if you want a lighter, more casual dinner.

Seafood · Smithfield
Fish Shop

Tiny counter-style spot in Smithfield. Seafood-focused, exceptional. Small — a party of six would need to book far ahead.

Pubs beyond the plan

The trad-music trio and the historic pours

Trad Music · Historic
The Cobblestone

Smithfield. The trad session pub — locals jamming, not performers. No food, no pretense. Saturday night is the play.

Trad Music · Iconic
O'Donoghues

Merrion Row (not the tourist trap on Suffolk St). The Dubliners played here. Cozy, packed on weekends, brilliant music. Great Friday option.

Best Guinness
Mulligan's

Poolbeg Street. Widely considered the best-poured Guinness in Dublin. Zero frills, unchanged for a century, perfect final-night pub.

Historic · Victorian
The Palace Bar

Fleet Street. Victorian-era interior, stained glass, cozy snugs. A writer's pub — Yeats, Kavanagh, Behan all drank here.

Oldest in Ireland
The Brazen Head

Since 1198. Touristy but the atmosphere genuinely earns it — Irish stew and a pint is the move. Good stop on the walk back from Cobblestone.

Trad Music · Alternative
Hughes' Bar

Chancery Street, near the Four Courts. Excellent trad sessions, fewer tourists than Cobblestone. Perfect if Cobblestone is packed.

Skip list

Things to save your time and money on

Skip
Most of Temple Bar

Overpriced and almost entirely tourists — pints hitting €10. Walk through, take the photo, then go elsewhere. The real Dublin pubs are 5 minutes away in every direction.

Skip
The O'Donoghues on Suffolk St

Not the famous one. Confusingly named. Go to O'Donoghues on Merrion Row instead — that's the one with the trad music heritage.

Skip
Glendalough as a day trip

Beautiful, but eats a whole day for what's essentially "another nice walk." Howth gives you 80% of the experience in half the time, with seafood at the end and no rental car needed.

Skip
Hop-on hop-off buses

Dublin is small and walkable. Six people in a taxi is €12 for any city trip. The buses are slow, expensive per person, and get stuck in traffic.
