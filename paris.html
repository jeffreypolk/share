The shape of it Three and a half days, two fixed points, and a lot of deliberate slack

You've got four nights and, realistically, three full days. The temptation with Paris is to schedule every hour of them. Don't. This guide gives each day two or three anchors — things with a time attached, usually because they're booked — and then leaves the rest open, with a short list of what's nearby if you want it. If you skip everything on the loose lists and sit in a café for two hours instead, the day still works.

The one real advantage you have is location. You're in the middle of the Marais, a few minutes from the river, and the jazz club is a six-minute walk from your front door. Almost nothing in this plan requires a long transit leg.

Saturday and Sunday are Heritage Days

The 43rd Journées européennes du patrimoine falls on 19–20 September, and over 1,500 venues open across Paris. Two consequences worth planning around.

The good: the Arc de Triomphe is free both days with no reservation, open 10:00 to 23:00. Sainte-Chapelle is free too.

The bad: central Paris will be conspicuously busier, and "free Sainte-Chapelle" means a queue that eats your morning. That's why every ticketed indoor sight in this plan lands on Thursday or Friday, and Saturday stays outdoors and flexible.

Centre Pompidou is shut. It's a five-minute walk from you, so it's worth knowing before you wander over. It closed in September 2025 for a €460m renovation and doesn't reopen until 2030. The Bourse de Commerce near Les Halles is the nearest equivalent if you want modern art.

Catacombs — Thursday 17th. Book at 10:00 Paris time on Thursday 10 September

This one has its own rules and they're unforgiving. There are no tickets at the door — none. No ticket office exists. Arriving without a pre-booked timed slot means you are not admitted, and only 200 people are allowed underground at a time.

The official window opens seven days ahead at 10:00 Paris time, which is 04:00 in New York. Set the alarm for Thursday the 10th. Around €31 an adult with the audio guide. The Paris Museum Pass is not valid here.

If official slots are gone, GetYourGuide and Tiqets hold separate guided-tour allocations released further ahead, from about €40. Worth checking catacombes.paris.fr now in case more than seven days is bookable.

The cruise, in detail

Where and when
Port de la Conférence, 8e
Métro line 9 to Alma-Marceau, then two minutes down to the quay. Leave the flat around 19:00 to be there comfortably at 19:45. Coming home, line 9 back to Franklin D. Roosevelt and change to line 1, which runs straight to Saint-Paul. Roughly 30 minutes, and the last trains are well after midnight.
What to wear
Smart dress, evenings
Jeans are fine here — the ban is on sportswear, flipflops, bermuda shorts and baseball caps, and denim isn't on the list. Dark, clean, unripped, with a collared shirt or jacket and leather shoes. Chinos are what you'll actually see more of on board if you'd rather blend in.
What you'll see
Two and a quarter hours
Upstream past the Louvre, the Conciergerie and the Hôtel de Ville, turning near the Institut du Monde Arabe and coming back down the narrow channel beside the Île de la Cité for a close pass at Notre-Dame, then on past the Orsay to the Eiffel Tower. Sunset is around 19:50, so you sail in the dark — but you get the tower sparkle at 21:00 and 22:00 from the water, and the boat has a full open-air top deck for it.
Skip the Paris Museum Pass. With this itinerary you'd roughly break even, and you'd still have to make separate timed reservations for the Louvre anyway. Booking each thing direct is simpler and no more expensive.
16 Wednesday One booked hour, and a free afternoon around it

The earlier train gives you an afternoon with a single thing in it. Bags down by half two, then nothing until you need to be at a door on the Île de la Cité at quarter past four. Don't fill that gap with a monument — you've got a travel day behind you and three more days ahead. Use it to walk the neighbourhood you're going to be living in.

One hard rule: bags go to the flat first. Maximum bag size at the chapel entrance is 40 × 40 × 20 cm and there is no storage on site.

Off the train
No arrival controls — those were done in London — so you walk straight off. Line 4 to Châtelet, change to line 1, off at Saint-Paul. About 35 minutes door to door. Skip the taxi; afternoon traffic on Sébastopol is worse than the change.
14:40
Check in, unpack properly
You have an hour and a half before you need to leave, so there's no reason to rush this. Coffee somewhere on Rue des Rosiers when you're done — you're two minutes from it.

16:30

Fifteen minutes in the lower chapel, forty-five in the upper. Go up close to the rose window at the west end, which is where the late light lands, and give the south wall time — those seven windows are the ones being lit directly at this hour. Out by half five with hours of daylight left.
20:00whenever
Dinner, close to home
Nothing that needs a reservation or a métro ride. See the eating section — Chez Janou and Café des Musées are both a short walk and both forgiving of a late-ish arrival.

Filling the gaps, either side

  • Before: the Marais on foot. Rue des Rosiers, then Rue Vieille du Temple north, then the Village Saint-Paul courtyards five minutes from your door — linked cobbled yards full of antique dealers that most visitors walk straight past.
  • After: Île Saint-Louis. You come out at half five with the light still good. Cross over and take Rue Saint-Louis-en-l'Île end to end, nine minutes from the flat and the best introduction to the city you'll get.
  • Berthillon, on that same street. The ice cream. Any café along the row serves it if the main shop has a queue.
  • Place des Vosges for a first drink — eight minutes from the flat, arcaded on all four sides, and the right place to sit at seven o'clock.
  • Go back out after dinner. Pont de la Tournelle gives you Notre-Dame's flying buttresses lit from below, which beats the view from the front.
  • A nightcap on Rue Vieille du Temple. The Marais stays up. Le Mary Celeste does oysters and cocktails late.
17 Thursday Notre-Dame, the Catacombs, jazz — and gaps between all three

Thursday absorbs the Catacombs neatly. Notre-Dame in the late morning, lunch on the Left Bank, then a straight run south on line 4 to Denfert-Rochereau. Two very different undergrounds in one day and a cellar to finish in, which is either a theme or a coincidence.

It's still not a full day. There's an hour either side of lunch with nothing in it, and the whole stretch from late afternoon to dinner is yours.

11:30
Notre-Dame free slot, booked 15 Sep
Thirteen minutes' walk. Allow fifteen minutes for security on top of your slot. The restored interior is the surprise — eight centuries of soot came off with the repairs, and the stone is pale in a way nobody alive had seen it. Shoulders and knees covered. An hour inside is plenty.
14:10
Line 4 south to Denfert-Rochereau
Direct from Cité, about ten minutes, no change. Bring a layer — it's a constant 14°C down there whatever the weather is doing above. Bags must be 40 × 30 × 20 cm or smaller and there's no cloakroom, because the exit is 700 m from the entrance.
14:30or 15:00
Catacombs book 10 Sep, 10:00 Paris
Arrive 15 minutes early; late arrivals are refused, and the ticket is only valid for its slot. It's 131 steps down and 112 back up, a 1.5 km one-way circuit, and about 45 minutes to an hour at the pace they set. There's no shortening it once you're in. You enter at 1 Avenue du Colonel Henri Rol-Tanguy and come out at 21 bis Avenue René-Coty — restrooms are at the exit, not the entrance.
19:00
Café des Musées booked
49 Rue de Turenne, nine minutes from the flat. Continuous service from 10:00 to 22:30. Small room, quick service at this hour — but 1h40 is two courses, not three. The escargots in mushroom caps and the bread are what everyone agrees on; the beef bourguignon divides people. Skip dessert and have your last drink at the club. Worth a call to remind them you have a 21:30 show.
20:40
Walk to 38Riv
About 700 m, ten minutes, southwest through the Marais to Rue de Rivoli. No métro needed.
20:50
38Riv show at 21:30
A cellar with about 30 seats, side by side, front row a metre from the piano. Regulars say arrive fifteen minutes early even with a reservation if you care where you sit. Coat check at street level, and a bar at the back with a video feed of the band, so being early isn't dead time. No food served — drinks only, self-service.

The gaps — pick one or two, not six

  • Lunch on a Left Bank side street after the cathedral. Anywhere off Boulevard Saint-Germain. The rule is simple: no menu in six languages, no photographs of the food.
  • Shakespeare and Company. Directly across from the cathedral, and on your way to the métro. Crowded and worth it anyway; the reading room upstairs is free and quiet.
  • Montparnasse, after you surface. You come out in the 14th, so the Tour Montparnasse observation deck is fifteen minutes north — the one Paris view that includes the Eiffel Tower, and far quieter than the tower itself.
  • Or Jardin du Luxembourg on the way back, line 4 to Odéon. Take one of the green metal chairs by the fountain and stay an hour. This is the correct use of a Paris afternoon.
  • Rue Mouffetard if you'd rather have a market street than a park — properly old, climbing away from the Panthéon.
  • Stay in the Marais instead and skip the Left Bank entirely: Musée Carnavalet, the history of Paris, free permanent collection, ten minutes from the flat.
Whatever you do with the afternoon, be back at the flat by seven. It's a late show and you'll want to have sat down before it.
18 Friday Two museums, then a late night on the water

The boat leaving at 20:30 rather than at sunset turns out to work in your favour. It means you don't have to go straight from a museum to a dining room in the clothes you've been walking in all day — there's time to come back to the flat, sit down for an hour, and change properly. Build that in. It's the difference between arriving at the pier fresh and arriving at it frazzled.

One honest warning about the Louvre: you cannot see it. Pick two or three things you actually want, walk to them, and leave when you're bored. Three hours is a full visit. Five is a forced march.

09:00

17:45
Back to the flat

19:00
Leave for the pier
Line 1 west to Franklin D. Roosevelt, change to line 9, off at Alma-Marceau. Around 30 minutes plus the walk down to the water. Port de la Conférence, on the Right Bank just east of the Pont de l'Alma.
19:45
Boarding booked · sails 20:30
Doors open 45 minutes before departure and seating is assigned on arrival, so being early is worth an actual better table by the window. Back alongside at 22:45; home by about 23:20.

The gap between them

  • Lunch at Palais-Royal. Five minutes from the Louvre exit, and the garden behind the colonnade is a genuinely peaceful courtyard in the middle of the first arrondissement.
  • Galerie Vivienne. A glass-roofed 1820s shopping arcade with mosaic floors, two minutes further. Takes ten minutes to walk and is worth every one.
  • Walk the Tuileries from the Louvre to Place de la Concorde, then cross the Seine on the Pont Royal straight to the Orsay's front door. That's your transit and your sightseeing in one.
  • The Orangerie, at the far end of the Tuileries, if you want a third museum — two oval rooms of Monet's water lilies and not much else. Forty minutes, and a better experience than most of the Louvre.
  • If you'd rather not double back, skip going home and walk the Left Bank quais west from the Orsay to the tower instead — about 45 minutes along the river. Only do this if you've dressed for the boat that morning.
  • Eat something at four. Dinner won't reach the table until well after 21:00. A pastry and a coffee somewhere between the Orsay and the métro will save you.
19 Saturday One booked thing, then whatever you feel like

Deliberately the loosest day. It's the busiest day of your trip citywide because of Heritage Days, so the plan is to get the one ticketed thing done early and then stay outdoors and unhurried. If you end up doing nothing after lunch but sitting in the Marais, that's a good Saturday.

Free all day for Heritage Days, no reservation, open until 23:00. Take the underpass, never the roundabout. The roof looks down all twelve avenues at once and is the better Paris view of the two — you can see the tower from it, which you can't from the tower.

End close to home. Note that a good number of places on and around Rue des Rosiers close from Friday evening through Saturday — including L'As du Fallafel and Miznon — so plan a street or two over.

Ways to fill the middle

  • Montmartre in the late afternoon. Métro to Abbesses. Sacré-Cœur steps at golden hour, but the real pleasure is the streets north and west of the basilica where nobody goes. Skip Place du Tertre.
  • Or drop the whole west side and spend the afternoon on Heritage Days openings instead — the programme lists buildings that are shut the other 363 days of the year. Check paris.fr the week before and pick one near you.
  • Marché des Enfants Rouges, ten minutes from the flat. Oldest covered market in Paris, open Saturday, and a good low-effort lunch if you come back early.
  • Sit in the Village Saint-Paul courtyards, five minutes from your door — antique dealers around a set of linked cobbled yards that most visitors walk straight past.

Basics Small things that save an argument

Getting around

Tap in with a contactless card or phone; there's no need for paper tickets or a Navigo unless you want one. Saint-Paul on line 1 is three minutes from the door, and line 1 alone gets you to the Louvre, Concorde, the Champs-Élysées and the Arc de Triomphe without changing. For the Eiffel Tower take line 1 to Charles de Gaulle–Étoile and change to line 6, which runs above ground across the Pont de Bir-Hakeim — a better arrival than coming up from underground.

One caveat on that route: between September and November 2026, line 6 is suspended on certain Sundays between Charles de Gaulle–Étoile and La Motte-Picquet-Grenelle for modernization work, which closes Bir-Hakeim station. Your tower morning is a Saturday so it shouldn't apply, but it's worth thirty seconds on the tower's news page the night before. If it is affected, line 8 to École Militaire is the fallback.

The Jewish quarter, on Saturdays

Rue des Rosiers and the streets around it are the historic Pletzl, and a good number of shops and restaurants close from Friday evening until Saturday evening. It's your immediate neighbourhood, so it's worth knowing on Saturday when you're hungry. Everything a few streets west is open as normal.

Sundays

Most of Paris shuts on Sunday. The Marais is one of the few districts where shops stay open, which is a genuine perk of where you're staying — and slightly wasted on you, since you'll be at the airport. If you want anything from the neighbourhood, buy it Saturday.

Dining rhythm

Kitchens generally run 19:30 to 22:30 and a 20:00 table is normal, not late. Service is included in the price by law, so tipping is rounding up or leaving a few euros for something good — not a percentage. Tap water is free and correct to order: une carafe d'eau.

Things worth pre-empting

Download the Notre-Dame app now so the account exists when the reservation window opens on the 15th. Save your Louvre, Orsay, Sainte-Chapelle, Eiffel and Bateaux Mouches QR codes offline — mobile signal at the Île de la Cité entrance is unreliable, which is exactly where you'll need it first. Put Port de la Conférence in your phone rather than trusting yourselves to remember it at 19:30 on the night.
